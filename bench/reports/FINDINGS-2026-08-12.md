# Findings & recommendation — local model/quant on the GTX 1070

Companion to the data report [`ollama-perf-2026-08-12.md`](./ollama-perf-2026-08-12.md).
Box: GTX 1070 (8 GB, Pascal CC 6.1), driver 550.54.14, ollama 0.6.8. Six models
measured, one resident at a time, prompts mirroring the app's routing profiles.

## Headline numbers

| Model | VRAM | On GPU? | Gen tok/s | Long answer (400 tok) | Cold load |
|---|--:|---|--:|--:|--:|
| `qwen2.5:3b-instruct-q4_K_M` | 3.1 GB | 100% | **43.2** | ~9.6 s | 1.9 s |
| `qwen2.5:7b-instruct-q4_K_M` | 6.0 GB | 100% | **26.1** | ~15.6 s | 4.1 s |
| `llama3.1:latest` (current) | 6.9 GB | 100% | 25.5 | ~12.8 s* | 6.2 s |
| `qwen2.5:7b-instruct-q5_K_M` | 6.7 GB | 100% | 25.3 | ~16.4 s | 4.9 s |
| `qwen2.5:7b-instruct-q6_K` | 7.5 GB | 100% | 20.9 | ~19.7 s | 5.4 s |
| `qwen2.5:14b-instruct-q3_K_M` | 9.4 GB | **88% (spill)** | 8.9 | ~47 s | 6.9 s |

\* llama3.1's grammar answer stopped at 317 tokens, so its wall-clock isn't directly
comparable to the 400-token qwen runs; per-token it's ~25 tok/s like the 7b qwens.

## What the data says

1. **The 14B doesn't fit — confirmed, not estimated.** At q3_K_M it needs 9.4 GB and
   ollama pushes 12% of layers to CPU (`size_vram 8.32 / size 9.4 GB`). The result is
   ~8.9 tok/s and a **47-second** grammar explanation — ~5× slower than the 7Bs. Off
   the table on this card. (Lower quant, e.g. q2_K, would fit but wrecks 14B quality.)

2. **Quant level barely moves quality but clearly moves speed.** Across the 7B:
   q4 → 26.1, q5 → 25.3, q6 → 20.9 tok/s, and footprint climbs 6.0 → 6.7 → 7.5 GB.
   q6 is ~20% slower and sits near the VRAM ceiling for no meaningful quality gain at
   7B. **q4_K_M is the sweet spot;** q5/q6 are not worth it here. The model *tier*
   (3B vs 7B) dominates both speed and quality far more than the quant level.

3. **qwen2.5:7b-q4 is a strict upgrade over the current llama3.1.** Slightly faster
   (26.1 vs 25.5 tok/s), ~1 GB smaller (6.0 vs 6.9 GB → more KV/context headroom),
   fully on GPU, and qwen2.5-7B is a stronger instruct model than llama3.1-8B on
   general benchmarks. No downside on this hardware.

4. **The 3B is genuinely fast.** 43 tok/s (1.7× the 7Bs), 3.1 GB, ~1.9 s cold load —
   snappy for live/interactive turns. The trade is model quality; fine for forgiving
   tasks (drills, examiner chat), weaker for graded writing feedback.

5. **First-token latency is a non-issue.** With the model kept resident
   (`OLLAMA_KEEP_ALIVE=-1`), TTFT is ~0.06–0.09 s everywhere except the 14B spill
   (~0.15 s). The only latency that bites is **long generations** — the tok/s column
   is what governs "feel."

## Recommendation

- **Default → `qwen2.5:7b-instruct-q4_K_M`.** Best quality that keeps current speed and
  frees VRAM. Set `OLLAMA_MODEL=qwen2.5:7b-instruct-q4_K_M` and restart the app.
- **If you want snappier interactive turns → `qwen2.5:3b-instruct-q4_K_M`** for the
  forgiving profiles, accepting lower quality on graded feedback.
- **Skip** q5_K_M, q6_K (no gain for the cost) and the 14B (spills, unusable).

### Optional: mix models per profile
`OLLAMA_MODEL` is one global tag, but `ai_routing.ollama.yaml` can hardcode a
different `ollama/<tag>` per profile — e.g. 3B for `drill_*`/`examiner_roleplay`
(latency-sensitive, forgiving) and 7B-q4 for `writing_feedback`/`vocab_*` (graded /
strict-JSON). Only a real win if you want to trade a little VRAM (both resident) or
accept reload lag when switching; noted as a follow-up, not applied.

## Reproduce
`bash bench/run_on_box.sh` on the box, then `python3 bench/render_report.py`. Raw
per-run samples are in `bench/results/*.json`.
